How It Works

The care athletes have always had. Built for everyone else.

Ask anyone who has been through a college training room what good orthopedic care feels like. Someone puts hands on you the day it happens. The people treating you talk to each other. You leave knowing what is wrong, what the plan is, and what comes next. Nothing waits on an approval.

That model was never a secret. It has simply been reserved for people whose livelihood depends on movement — and we think yours does too. Longevity is meaningless without the ability to move, so we treat the patient and the exam rather than the image alone.

DirectOrtho+ is that room, built for everyone else — precision musculoskeletal medicine, sports medicine, and non-surgical orthopedic care under one roof. Our clinicians spent nearly three decades in orthopedics, and every member of this team was chosen for their philosophy as much as their credentials. Direct pay is what makes it possible: when the cost is settled up front, the only question left in the room is what your body actually needs.

The patient is always the most important person in the room.

That sentence governs how we schedule, how long we spend with you, how we decide, and how we follow up. Direct pay isn't a billing strategy. It's what keeps the care centered on you instead of the billing code.

05 · Injections & Orthobiologics

Biologics where the evidence supports them — and honesty where it doesn't.

When an injection is the right call, it happens that day, in that room, performed by the clinician who just examined you. Ultrasound guidance is used wherever it improves accuracy, and it is included in the price rather than billed as a separate procedure by a separate entity.

Your own blood carries the growth factors, platelets and protective proteins that tendons and joints use to heal. Orthobiologics concentrate them and place them precisely where the injury is. In appropriately selected patients — the right preparation, ultrasound guidance and rehab afterward — they may improve pain and function in certain tendon and joint conditions. Done carelessly, they are an expensive injection.

PRP has its best evidence in tendinopathy — elbow, patellar, gluteal — and in mild to moderate knee osteoarthritis. A2M and autologous protein concentrate are considered in selected osteoarthritis cases. We will tell you plainly when we think a biologic is a reasonable option for your problem, and just as plainly when we don't think it is worth your money.

How it is done here

A biologic is a decision that follows an exam, never one that precedes it.

  • Evaluation first. An exam and imaging decide whether a biologic is a reasonable option for your problem — never the other way around.
  • Closed, sterile, single-use preparation. Prepared in the room from your own blood, used the same day, and placed under ultrasound guidance.
  • Rehab is part of the procedure. A biologic without a loading program is half a treatment. Physical therapy is down the hall and planned before you are injected.
  • Outcomes tracked. Pain and function measured before and after, so the decision about a second injection is made on your numbers rather than a hunch.

What we will not do

  • Sell a series up front. One injection, then a reassessment.
  • Offer products the evidence does not support, or describe anything here as a "stem cell" treatment.
  • Promise a result. Response varies by person and by condition, and we will go through what the evidence does and does not show before you decide.

A Word on Orthopedic Urgent Care

Urgent care is built to triage you. This is built to finish the question.

We say that with some standing: the clinician who leads DirectOrtho+ helped establish orthopedic urgent care in Athens, twice. For a Saturday ankle, an orthopedic walk-in center is genuinely better than an emergency room and better than a general urgent care, and on the right night it is exactly where you should go.

It is simply engineered for a different job. A triage visit ends in a disposition — where you go next. A DirectOrtho+ visit ends in a decision — what is wrong, what it needs, and what happens now.

When Surgery Is Needed

Most orthopedic problems never need surgery. When yours does, we will send you to the right hands.

Conservative-first doesn't mean surgery-never. It means surgery is reserved, not rushed — and when the diagnosis genuinely calls for it, the question becomes which surgeon, for this specific problem, for you.

Over nearly three decades in Athens, our clinicians have worked alongside almost every orthopedic surgeon in this city. We know who has the best hands for a particular shoulder, knee, or spine — and who we would trust with our own families.

We don't hand you a directory. We hand you two or three names, matched to your diagnosis, and we tell you why each one is on that short list.

  • Matched to the diagnosis. The right surgeon for a rotator cuff isn't always the right surgeon for a knee.
  • Personally vetted. Surgeons we know from decades of practice — not names pulled from an insurance directory.
  • Provider-to-provider. A direct conversation surgeon to clinician. You don't carry paper records between offices or repeat your story from scratch.
  • Insurance activates where it belongs. Your coverage steps in for surgery, advanced imaging and hospital care — MRI, CT and DEXA ordered and tracked with clinical follow-through.
  • This stays your orthopedic home. Before, during and after any procedure the relationship continues — and post-operative rehab is down the hall, from $75.

Questions People Actually Ask

The things you'd ask on the phone, answered here first.

Do I need a referral?

No. No referral, no prior authorization, no gatekeeper. Call or text 706-224-9951 and we will schedule you — usually same day or next day.

Do you take insurance?

We don't bill it. DirectOrtho+ is direct-pay, so you pay a published price at the time of service and there is no claim, no explanation of benefits, and no bill arriving six weeks later. Visits are HSA and FSA eligible, and we provide an itemized superbill on request if you want to submit it to your insurer yourself. Your insurance still does the job it is genuinely good at — surgery, hospital care, advanced imaging — and we coordinate the handoff when you get there.

Is this actually cheaper than urgent care or the ER?

For most orthopedic problems, yes. A FASTPassOrtho+ visit is $329 with on-site imaging included when indicated. Published self-pay ranges for orthopedic urgent care in this market generally run $250 to $600 before separate imaging and radiology bills, and national self-pay data puts a typical emergency department visit well above $2,000. Your own numbers will depend on your plan, your deductible and the facility. The honest framing is this: before your deductible is met, you are paying out of pocket either way. The only question is whether you know the number before you walk in.

Is imaging really included?

Digital X-ray and diagnostic ultrasound performed in our office are included in the new patient and FASTPassOrtho+ visit prices whenever they are clinically indicated. Your images are reviewed with you in the room by the clinician who just examined you, so you leave with the findings rather than waiting on a report. If a case calls for a formal radiology over-read, we will tell you before it is ordered and what it costs. Advanced imaging like MRI is performed at an outside facility and billed by that facility, and we will tell you what it should cost and where to get it done well.

Can my child be seen for a sports injury or a possible concussion?

Yes. A youth sports injury evaluation is $199 on a weekday, $249 same-day, and $299 in the Saturday injury window. A suspected concussion evaluation is $249, or $299 urgent or weekend. Under Georgia's Return to Play Act, a physician assistant practicing under physician supervision with documented concussion training is a qualifying health care provider. Any red flag — worsening headache, repeated vomiting, seizure, a focal neurological deficit, escalating confusion — belongs in an emergency department immediately, and we will tell you that without hesitating.

Is there a student rate?

Yes. Any currently enrolled college or graduate student with a valid student ID, from any school, pays the youth rate: $199 for a weekday injury evaluation, $249 same-day, $299 in the Saturday injury window, and $99 for a follow-up ($149 with imaging). The student rate covers visits; injections, therapy, bracing and memberships are at the published prices.

Will you clear my athlete to play?

Only when they've earned it. You are paying for expert evaluation and honest management, never for a clearance decision made in advance. We follow a graduated return-to-learn and return-to-sport progression, and the timeline is set by how your athlete actually recovers — not by the schedule, the playoffs, or what anyone would prefer to hear.

If I do a virtual visit and still need to come in, am I paying twice?

No. If the same concern needs an in-office exam within 72 hours, the full virtual fee comes off that visit and you pay only the balance. A $159 after-hours consult followed by a $259 new patient visit means you owe $100. The credit applies to the professional visit fee — not to bracing, medication, injection supplies, outside imaging or lab work.

What if it turns out I need surgery?

Then you need surgery, and we will say so. We do not own an operating room, and nothing in this practice earns more when you have one — which is worth knowing about any surgical advice you receive in this town, including ours. We give you two or three surgeons matched to your specific diagnosis, tell you why each is on that list, and hand off provider to provider with your workup already complete. Your insurance activates for the procedure itself, and this remains your orthopedic home before and after.

Do I have to be a patient here to use physical therapy?

No. Our therapists practice at the full scope of their license, so you can start directly with a therapist for a defined number of visits without an office visit first. You can also bring a plan from another provider and we will execute it. Physical therapy is $100 for a 45-minute one-on-one session, and the Recovery Zone is $40.

Why are some prices going up after six months?

Because our founding prices are genuinely below where this practice needs to sit, and we would rather tell you that now than quietly reprice later. A new patient visit moves from $259 to $279, FASTPassOrtho+ from $329 to $349, and CompleteOrtho+ from $359 to $379 after our first six months. Follow-ups, imaging, procedures, therapy and virtual visits do not change.

What if I'm not sure which service I need?

Call or text 706-224-9951 and describe what happened. Sorting that out is our job, not yours — and we would rather spend three minutes routing you correctly than have you buy the wrong visit.
